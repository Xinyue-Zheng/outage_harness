The five rules of the reference policy, one picture each

Every picture is the same map: the site at the centre, the twelve direction bins, settlements and corridors as blocks, the study-area edge as a dashed line. Grey blocks are not fetched; blue are fetched; red marks locations where an outage sector is listed at -110 dBm or better; green marks fetched locations with no outage sector. The orange element is what the rule does. The policy applies the first rule whose condition holds, in this order, once per round.

Rule 1. backup_load names cells → one KPI call per technology

Condition

The completion check's backup_load is not met and lists cells with no baseline: the outage sectors, and every backup seen in the same sample as an outage sector at a covered location.

Action

Query all of them at once, one call per technology (LTE, NR), with the window the check computed: the outage start moved back four weeks to the outage end moved back one week, so every outage hour on the same weekday is covered.

Why first

It is the cheapest open item (one or two tool calls, no point calls) and the only one the model got wrong by splitting it over many rounds. The reference never splits it.

Effect

backup_load becomes met, or lists the cells whose query returned no rows as missing, which does not block.

Rule 2. direction open, area named → fetch the nearest such area

Condition

For some outage sector and some direction bin, the check reports open with an area to fetch: either activity locations within the sector's reach are unfetched, or the nearest activity area beyond the reach in that bin is not yet fully fetched. The check names the area.

Action

Fetch that area. When several directions are open, take the area whose nearest location is closest to the site; ties by area id. Here the corridor R02 comes before the settlement S02.

Why before expansion

Closure inside the study area is always cheaper than growing it, and an unfetched area inside the reach must be fetched for the report in any case.

Effect

The direction closes if the sector is absent there; or the reach grows, and the next area beyond becomes the one named; or a neighbouring bin becomes active.

Rule 3. direction open at the edge → expand it by 2 km

Condition

The check reports a direction open at the study-area edge: the sector covers out to the edge and no activity location with a sample lies beyond it inside the study area. No rule-2 item exists anywhere (rule 2 is checked first).

Action

Expand the study area in that direction by a fixed 2 km: the wedge of the bin plus its margins, from the current edge outward along every bearing. The program fetches OSM for the wedge's bounding box and derives the settlements and corridors there as new areas. If several directions are open at the edge, the one whose sector is strongest in the outermost strip goes first.

Why fixed

The model may choose a step from the edge evidence; the reference does not judge, it steps by 2 km until an activity location beyond the reach appears or the frame cap is reached.

Effect

Either a new area lies beyond the reach and the direction becomes open with that area named (rule 2 next round), or the wedge holds no activity and the direction stays open at the new edge (rule 3 again), until the frame edge closes it at the cap, which the report records.

Rule 4. unobserved, activity inside → fetch the nearest area there

Condition

A direction bin has no fetched location within 2 km of the site, and an activity area has locations in that bin inside the study area. With the probe ring this is rare: it happens when the probe's points in that bin fell outside the frame or their calls failed.

Action

Fetch the nearest activity area in that bin.

Why it exists

The check only asks about directions where a sector has been seen; a bin never looked at can hide a sector. Fetching once there turns "never looked" into "looked, sector present" or "looked, nothing".

What it does not do

A bin that is unobserved with no activity area in it is left alone: there is nowhere people are in that direction. The report lists it as never observed, with that reason.

Rule 5. nothing open → verification pass, then end

Condition

No rule 1 to 4 applies: backup_load is met, every checked direction is closed, no direction is open at the edge, no unobserved bin has activity. This is where the harness would accept a finish.

Action

Once, for every closed direction of every sector: fetch the next activity area beyond the closing area in that bin, if one exists inside the study area. If the sector is absent there too, the closure is confirmed. If the sector covers there, a served pocket lay past the first absent area (a blocked signal returning), the direction reopens and rules 2 and 3 take over next round. When every direction is confirmed, the run ends: redistribution, report, reference files.

Why the reference does this and the harness does not

The harness stops at one absent area, the "one absent area is enough" decision. The reference pays for one more so that the ground truth is not fooled by a pocket; the difference between the two is then measurable as extent recall.

How a run of the policy reads, round by round

Round 1 after the probe: rule 2 fetches the settlement around the site. Round 2: rule 1 queries the KPI for the sectors and backups seen so far. Rounds 3 to k: rule 2 fetches the nearest named area each round, rule 1 again whenever a new backup appears, rule 3 when a direction reaches the edge. Rule 4 only if a probe bin failed. Rule 5 once at the end, and once more after any reopening. Every round is one action, logged with its latency, so the reference's cost is the sum.
