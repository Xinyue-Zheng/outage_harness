Fixed outage investigation skill

Purpose
Investigate a planned EnodeB outage using the tool outputs returned so far in this conversation.
The task interval describes a planned outage, not proof that it happened. Current wall time does not change that meaning. Keep mapped geography, measured historical coverage, historical KPI baselines, and predicted impact separate.

Evidence and scope
Only queried locations with explicitly valid records establish coverage. Missing, unqueried, and other or unknown statuses remain unknown. A queried location with no sample has missing data; it does not show absence of coverage. Other-cells-only coverage differs from coverage containing an outage sector. Do not invent signal measurements.
For a planned outage, classify cell IDs by prefix: three letters plus five digits (for example CCL06069) is LTE; four letters plus six digits (for example CCSN016069) is NR. Every observed sector matching an explicitly supplied outage prefix is an outage cell. None is an available backup. LTE outage sectors can only have LTE backup candidates; NR outage sectors can only have NR backup candidates. When both LTE and NR outage prefixes are explicitly supplied, assess each technology separately; do not infer that they belong to the same site.
Candidate cells need observed coverage evidence; presence alone does not establish usable backup load.
Coverage is fetched one point call per analysis grid location of the chosen area. Each call returns the measurement samples within the resolution radius of that location. Every sample lists its serving sector and the strongest neighbour cells, each with RSRP, so the cells in one sample were observed together at one measurement: they support co-occurrence and a strongest-cell comparison there. A cell that is not listed at a location is not shown to be absent there, because a sample lists only the serving sector and a few strongest neighbours. Unknown or absent fields remain unknown.
Land-use tags indicate where users are likely, not measured demand. Known boundaries do not establish coverage. Overlapping area counts are not additive.
A road centerline is not an area; its declared buffer corridor is the query area.
Query-boundary statistics concern queried locations facing an unqueried interior, not a signal contour. Missing boundary data does not establish a coverage edge.

Area importance
An outage matters where people use the network.
- Work outward from the down site. Distance comes first: the outage sectors serve the area around the site, so a near area matters more than a far area of the same or a smaller kind. A hamlet 3 km away comes before a village 10 km away.
- Activity areas (A##) are where mapped human activity is: residential, commercial, industrial and retail land use, buildings, amenities, shops and leisure features lying within 200 m of each other. Their description lists the original OpenStreetMap tags and counts; more buildings, amenities and shops mean more users. Start with the activity area that contains or lies nearest the down site, then the nearest activity areas with many features.
- Settlement areas (S##) are discs drawn around mapped place points by a radius rule (500 m to 3 km); they show that a settlement exists, not where its users are. Where an activity area overlaps a settlement, the activity area locates the users. Where a settlement has no mapped activity area, its disc is the only evidence of users there.
- Road corridors (highways and major roads) carry travellers who use the network. A corridor that passes within a few kilometres of the site, or crosses a settlement near the site, is as important as a nearby settlement; query it directly rather than relying on the edges of other queries.
- Farmland, grassland, forest, water and other unpopulated land have few or no users; losing service there is normally acceptable, so the program does not offer them as areas. They appear only as land-use shares in descriptions.
Distance and land use rank areas for investigation; they do not establish measured coverage or demand.

Choosing an action
Choose one necessary, actionable information gap and the least costly declared action that can resolve it. Explain why the result matters in purpose and in the gap question. The tool outputs are the evidence; keep track of what was queried and what it showed.
Do not invent area, cell, observation IDs, coordinates, bounds, or tool names.
Use only parameters supported by the available declarations. If a required bound or identifier is absent, report that gap rather than fabricate a query.
Judge each action by the efficiency of the whole run, not only the next step: the investigation should reach a supported conclusion with as little total processing time and storage as possible. A coverage query costs one point call for every analysis grid location of the area that has not been queried yet. Locations already queried are never fetched again, so overlapping areas cost only their new locations.
The resolution values the coverage tool accepts are listed with the action; the resolution is the search radius in metres around each location. A larger radius returns more samples per location and more complete cell lists, but neighbouring locations may then share samples; a smaller radius returns fewer samples and more locations with missing data.
Prefer the smallest area that can answer the question; query a larger area only when the cheaper evidence leaves the question open. Choose the radius by the question: a small radius to locate where a sector is served, a larger radius to see which cells are listed together at the locations of an area. Longer KPI windows, more cells, and finer temporal resolution increase KPI cost. The current KPI tools return hourly data; do not request unsupported 15-minute sampling or extra parameters. KPI technology and backup eligibility follow the cell IDs: an ID that starts with four letters and six digits (for example CCSN003553) is an NR cell for query_nr_rrc_prb; an ID that starts with three letters and five digits (for example CCL06069) is an LTE cell for query_lte_rrc_prb. The program routes each cell to the matching KPI tool and splits a mixed list into both queries. Only query backup cells whose radio technology matches an explicitly supplied outage prefix; mixed LTE/NR backup candidates are allowed only when both outage technologies were explicitly supplied.

KPI baseline
Estimate each outage hour's expected KPI from the same weekday and hours in previous weeks. Use several windows, for example W1 to W4 (about one month); a single prior week is not a baseline. If you query any window that is not built from the listed windows, give the reason in purpose. Query the outage cells and the backup candidates. The program keeps only the matching weekday and hours and averages each hour across the weeks; that per-hour average is the estimate for the same hour of the outage. Explain missing weeks and comparability. Respect program validation restrictions, read rejections and empty results before retrying, and do not repeat a window that returned no rows. Do not interpret KPI rows from a future planned interval as measured outage data.

Inspection and completion
Use an observation inspection action only if it is actually declared. Inspection notes and reviewer concerns are evidence or questions, not substituted decisions.
Use current final_report_inputs exactly for final_step_report when available.
A generated report does not prove that boundary or backup-load checks passed.
The boundary check is satisfied for each observed outage sector only when fully queried, valid coverage in a settlement or road corridor farther from the down site than the farthest settlement/road area where that sector was observed shows the sector absent. Do not query farther settlements or roads once this evidence exists. Distance is an ordering proxy from the down site, not a propagation contour. Backup-load evidence remains unknown until a verified computation is implemented.
finish is a control proposal with empty parameters, not a data tool. Explain any remaining gaps, unknown coverage, unsupported estimates, and excluded locations.
Choose finish when the evidence answers the task.
Do not present synthetic formulas or uncomputed predictions as calibrated results.

Feedback
Read tool errors and rejected proposals before choosing the next action. Failed or empty queries are not coverage evidence. Do not repeat an unchanged rejected proposal without resolving its cause.

Decision format
Return exactly one JSON object, without Markdown, with these keys:
{"purpose":"Why this next action is necessary", "gap":{"targets":["the area or cell ids the question is about"], "question":"One actionable question"}, "action":{"function":"a declared tool name or finish", "parameters":{}}}
Use each tool's declared parameter schema. The program reads only gap.targets and rejects a decision when:
- a target is neither a listed area id nor a cell id seen in valid coverage (the down EnodeB ID counts as seen);
- an action other than finish or final_step_report names no target;
- a coverage or inspection action has a cell target, or an area target other than its area_id;
- a KPI action has an area target, or a cell target missing from its cell_ids;
- a coverage action names a resolution that is not one of the listed values;
- State already answers a target, an area with no unqueried location and no missing data, or a cell with RRC and PRB values for every hour requested.
For finish, the targets are the areas you leave with unqueried locations and the question gives the reason. Never use the reference example's three-line format.

Completion checks, applied when you choose finish:
- boundary: an outage sector covers a location when a sample lists it at or above -110 dBm, serving or not. In a checked bin, every settlement or road-corridor location nearer than the farthest covered one must be fetched, and the nearest settlement or road-corridor locations beyond it must be fetched and show the sector below the threshold or not listed. A bin with no such location beyond, inside the study area, is open at the study-area edge and is reported, not required. Missing data never blocks.
- backup_load: reported, not required. A backup cell without a load baseline is reported as such.
When the boundary check passes, the program writes the final report and the run ends; you do not call a report action. Only LTE cells are backups for LTE outage sectors; only NR cells are backups for NR outage sectors.
